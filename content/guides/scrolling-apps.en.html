How we chose

We grouped the options by the point where they intervene, rather than by claimed minutes saved. Think about the last unwanted session: what started it, what kept it going, and what would have made the end easier to notice?

Earnit publishes this guide and is included in the comparison. Picks are editorial recommendations by use case, based on official product information checked on October 26, 2026; they are not a hands-on performance ranking. Our comparison methodology.

1. Earnit — Putting an activity before a session

Earnit converts walking, focused study and on-device push-up counting into minutes for selected apps.

This is worth considering if your break begins before you have decided to take it. Choose an earning action in advance and spend only the session you intended. The rule is easiest to evaluate when you can name the activity that should happen before the feed.

When to consider another option: If your main requirement is coordinated blocking on a computer and phone, compare Freedom first. For an iPhone routine, check iOS 18 compatibility and whether earning access fits better than a fixed allowance.

Official product details: Earnit

2. one sec — Reconsidering the first opening

one sec offers app-opening interventions and scheduled blocking.

Focus on this approach when the difficult decision happens in the first second: your hand opens an app without a purpose. Decide what a purposeful opening would look like before trying it. That gives you a clearer test than asking whether an app feels strict enough.

When to consider another option: If you need a period protected regardless of whether you try opening an app, compare the schedule settings. If you want effort to determine your next allowance, compare that earning condition rather than judging only the interruption.

Official product details: one sec

3. ScreenZen — Keeping a daily boundary

ScreenZen offers schedules and daily limits through a donation-supported app.

Consider a daily boundary if you are comfortable with some scrolling but want a ceiling. Select the apps behind the unwanted sessions rather than every app on the phone. Review the boundary after several ordinary days, including one day when your routine changes.

When to consider another option: Choose a limit when you want a defined allowance. If your goal is to finish an activity before a break, evaluate that condition separately; a quantity of available time and a prerequisite answer different questions.

Official product details: ScreenZen

4. Unrot — Connecting access to a chosen task

Unrot exchanges completed tasks for points that unlock selected apps.

This direction fits when the displaced activity is easy to name: reading a chapter, finishing a chore, or completing another chosen task. Make the task specific enough that “done” means something. A reward system is harder to assess when its prerequisite stays vague.

When to consider another option: If counting physical reps is your deciding requirement, verify the current task-checking method before choosing. Compare the camera-counting workflows in PushBlock or PushUp Time as well as the task menu.

Official product details: Unrot

Which one should you choose?

Choose one recurring moment, such as the first feed check after dinner. Keep the trial focused on that moment and review whether the opening and stopping decisions became clearer. For an app-specific setup, see how to limit Instagram.

Check current compatibility, available features and purchase terms before choosing. Earnit requires iOS 18 or later; current plans and trial eligibility are shown on the App Store.

A useful question before you install

Should I block every social app at once?

Start with the app responsible for the session you want to change. A smaller experiment makes it easier to tell whether the rule fits and which adjustment would help next.
