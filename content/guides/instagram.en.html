Set a daily Instagram limit

Start with the iPhone’s built-in controls if you want to give Instagram a fixed allowance each day. You don’t need to install Earnit to try this option.

  1. In Settings → Screen Time, open App Limits → Add Limit.
  2. Expand Instagram’s category and select Instagram individually.
  3. Choose a duration. Use Customize Days for different daily allowances, then save.

Selecting several apps shares the allowance between them. Apple documents this in its Screen Time guide for iOS 18. Labels can vary by iOS version.

Pick the boundary you want

The useful question is what should happen before your next session. Do you want Instagram to have a daily budget, or do you want to do something before you use it?

Neither choice needs to be a permanent verdict on Instagram. You’re choosing the condition for using it today. You can change the rule if it doesn’t fit how you actually use your phone.

Earn your next session

If “I’ll stop when the timer says so” isn’t the rule you want, try putting movement first. Earnit turns steps into minutes you can use on your selected apps.

  1. Choose the app you want to manage. In Earnit, grant Screen Time access and use the app selection controls.
  2. Connect Apple Health. Give Earnit permission to read your step count and set your exchange rate.
  3. Take a walk before your session. Spend the minutes you’ve earned. When your session ends, the selected apps are restricted again.

Make the rule practical

A useful boundary leaves room for the things you opened the app to do. Sending a message, sharing a photo, and watching a feed are different intentions, even when they happen in the same app.

Before a session, decide what you want from it. If you’re replying to a friend, finish that task before moving to the feed. If the feed is the thing you came for, choose a session length before starting.

You can also make the entry point less automatic. Move Instagram away from the first Home Screen, or decide on a place where you check it. Treat those as small experiments, not a promise that one trick will change your habits.

Start with one boundary. If you change five things at once, it’s harder to tell which one was useful. After a few days, keep, change, or remove the rule based on your experience.

Understand the limits

An app rule is not a guarantee against every way of using a service. If you also use Instagram in a browser, treat that as a separate route when deciding what to restrict. Don’t assume that choosing one app covers every website or device.

Earnit’s walking workflow depends on your Health and Screen Time permissions. It is a self-management tool, not an unchangeable lock or a promise of a particular result. For setup problems, use Earnit support.

Common questions

Do I need a paid app to try a daily limit?

You can start with the Screen Time controls already on your iPhone. Earnit offers a different rule: earning access through effort.

Does walking give me unlimited Instagram access?

No. In the workflow described here, steps earn a balance of minutes for sessions. It is not an unlimited day pass.

What should I do if I keep changing my rule?

Look at when you change it. A boundary that gets in the way of a necessary task may need a different selection or allowance. Choose a smaller experiment you can evaluate instead of continually tightening a rule you don’t use.
