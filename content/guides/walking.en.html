How walking becomes screen time

A daily app limit starts with an allowance and counts down. Earning screen time starts with something you do first. Your walk creates a balance; a deliberate app session spends it.

In Earnit, steps come from Apple Health and restrictions use Apple’s Screen Time controls. You choose the apps you want to manage. You can leave the rest of your phone available for the things you need.

Think of the minutes as a small budget for your next session. The point is to make opening an app a choice with a beginning and an end, rather than a decision you keep making while scrolling.

It’s a rule for selected apps, not a lock on your entire iPhone. Start with the app that most often turns a short break into a longer one.

Set up your walking rule

  1. Install Earnit on your iPhone. Check compatibility and current in-app purchase options on the App Store. Earnit requires iOS 18 or later.
  2. Connect your steps. Follow the permission prompts to let Earnit read your step count from Apple Health.
  3. Choose the apps to restrict. Allow Screen Time access and select the apps you want to manage.
  4. Choose your exchange rate. Set the relationship between steps and minutes in the app.
  5. Walk, then spend your minutes. Use the time you’ve earned for a session. When it ends, your selected apps are restricted again.

For a closer look at the mechanism, see how Earnit works. The homepage demonstration is illustrative, not a live connection to your step count.

Step goals or a balance of minutes?

“Block apps until I walk” can mean two different things. You might want every app to stay blocked until a single daily goal is complete. Or you might want movement to earn a limited amount of access along the way.

Earnit’s walking workflow is the second: steps become minutes that you spend on sessions. Don’t assume that reaching a target gives you unlimited access for the rest of the day.

An illustrative exchange: at 1,000 steps for 5 minutes, 2,000 steps would earn 10 minutes. This is an example, not a universal Earnit rate. Choose your rate in the app.

Choose a rule for your day

Before choosing numbers, name the moment you want to change. “I want a walk before my evening scroll” gives you something specific to try. A rule that is too broad is harder to judge.

  • Start with one distracting app so you can see whether the rule helps.
  • Keep the apps you need for work, travel, or staying in touch outside your selection.
  • Choose a walking routine that is comfortable and realistic for you. You don’t need to copy anyone else’s step target.
  • Review the experience after a few days. Adjust the exchange if it makes sessions too easy to open or impractical to earn.

If you mainly want a fixed daily cap, you may prefer a built-in app limit. Our guide to limiting Instagram on iPhone explains that option alongside earned minutes.

What to know before you start

Step reading and app restrictions depend on the permissions you grant. If a step count looks wrong, first compare it with Apple Health and check that Earnit has permission to read steps. If you need help, contact Earnit support.

This is a tool for managing your own app use. It is not a guarantee that you cannot change permissions, remove restrictions, or uninstall an app. No particular reduction in screen time is promised.

Common questions

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No separate wearable is required for the walking workflow described here. Earnit uses your step count in Apple Health on your iPhone.

Does the app pay me for walking?

The reward discussed here is access time for selected apps. It is not money, cryptocurrency, or an App Store credit.

What happens when a session ends?

The restrictions return to your selected apps. You need available earned minutes before starting another session.

Is the exchange rate fixed?

No. The step-to-minute figures on this website are illustrative. Choose a rate in Earnit that fits the routine you’re trying to build.
